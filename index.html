Abstract

We present From FDM to FNO, an open-source numerical kernel portfolio and interactive monograph designed for direct Ph.D. research and high-performance computing in computational physics and scientific machine learning (SciML). Beginning with explicit first-principles finite difference discretizations (FDM) for advection, diffusion, and incompressible Navier-Stokes projection methods, the curriculum systematically progresses to continuous implicit neural fields (PINN) and infinite-dimensional Fourier Neural Operators (FNO). All 20 canonical solver kernels are regression-verified against analytical benchmarks (Cole-Hopf, Fourier series) and empirical convergence rates ($p=1.00, p=2.01$), backed by 28 canonical textbook chapters, 34 zero-CDN offline lessons, and an automated daily arXiv research surveillance pipeline.

Curriculum & Volume Architecture

Five Canonical Volumes
Volume I · Chapters 01–10 12 Solver Kernels (FDM)

Foundations of Discrete Stencils & Classical Incompressible CFD

Literature Grounding: Barba & Forsyth (JOSS 2018) · LeVeque (SIAM 2007)

First-principles spatial and temporal discretizations on Cartesian meshes without black-box libraries. Derives characteristics, upwind causality, artificial numerical dissipation, Courant-Friedrichs-Lewy (CFL) stability bounds, Cole-Hopf analytical Burgers solutions, and Chorin's projection method for lid-driven cavity and channel flows.

  • Step 01: 1D Linear Advection & Upwind Causality FDM 01 · Upwind FTBS
  • Step 04: Viscous Burgers Equation vs. Exact Cole-Hopf Benchmark FDM 04 · Analytical L2
  • Steps 11–12: Incompressible Navier-Stokes & Ghia 1982 Cavity Benchmark FDM 11–12 · Chorin Projection
Volume II · Chapters 11–15 5 Solver Kernels (PINN)

Continuous Implicit Fields & Physics-Informed Neural Networks

Literature Grounding: Raissi et al. (JCP 2019) · Wang et al. (SISC 2021)

Transitions from discrete spatial grids to mesh-free coordinate neural networks. Covers higher-order PyTorch automatic differentiation (torch.autograd), gradient pathologies in multi-task loss optimization, trivial zero-solution collapse, and out-of-distribution (OOD) extrapolation limits.

  • Chapter 11: Continuous Implicit Neural Fields & Collocation Residuals PINN 01 · Autograd Graph
  • Chapter 15: Three-Curve Cross-Validation: Analytical vs. FDM vs. PINN PINN 05 · Rigorous Bounds
Volume III · Chapters 16–20 DeepXDE Framework Suite

Industrial Declarative PDE Frameworks & DeepXDE Architecture

Literature Grounding: Lu Lu et al. (SIAM Review 2021)

Explores production-grade declarative SciML frameworks. Analyzes geometry domains, spatio-temporal manifolds (GeometryXTime), Hessian diagonal indexing rules, point-set boundary conditions, and two-stage Adam-to-L-BFGS quasi-Newton refinement.

  • Chapter 16: DeepXDE Architecture & 1D Poisson Baseline DeepXDE 01 · Declarative API
  • Chapter 20: 2D Poisson Cross-Method Validation: FDM 5-Point vs. DeepXDE DeepXDE 05 · Pointwise L2
Volume IV · Chapters 21–25 3 Solver Kernels (FNO)

Operator Learning & Fourier Neural Operators (FNO)

Literature Grounding: Li, Kovachki, Stuart, Anandkumar (ICLR 2021)

A paradigm leap from solving a single PDE instance to mapping infinite-dimensional Banach function spaces. Handcrafts 1D spectral convolutions via real-valued FFTs (rFFT), assesses truncation-induced Gibbs phenomena, and evaluates zero-shot 4x super-resolution mesh independence.

  • Chapter 23: Spectral Convolution from Scratch: PyTorch rFFT & Mode Multiplication FNO 01 · Pure PyTorch
  • Chapter 25: Unified Closure: FDM Data Pipeline × FNO Operator Inference FNO 03 · Zero-Shot Generalization
Volume V · Chapters 26–28 Mathematical Foundations

Computational Science Mathematical Foundations: MIT 18.086

Literature Grounding: Gilbert Strang (Wellesley-Cambridge Press / MIT 18.086)

Linear algebra and stability theory underpinning all continuous simulations. Dissects the second-difference matrix -K, eigenvalue spectral distributions, and provides rigorous complex amplification proofs for von Neumann stability.

  • Chapter 27: Finite Difference Matrices, Eigenvalue Extremes & Stability Limits MIT 02 · Strang Algebra
  • Chapter 28: Von Neumann Stability Proof & Spectral Radius Analysis MIT 03 · Amplification Factor
